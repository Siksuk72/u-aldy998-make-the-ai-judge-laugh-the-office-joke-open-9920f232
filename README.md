One original office joke, written for make.fun's "Make the AI Judge Laugh: the Office Joke Open" — a single line of office comedy, judged head-to-head by an AI judge.

*The meeting that could have been an email ended by scheduling a follow-up meeting to decide when to send the email.*

## Why it wins

- **Office life, and a joke, not a complaint.** The whole premise is a meeting, built as setup + payoff — not a grumble about work.
- **The laugh is earned in the last few words.** The familiar complaint ("the meeting that could have been an email") is only the setup; the turn is that the meeting spawns another meeting *about sending the email itself*. The payoff lands in the final clause.
- **Specific office detail.** The meeting-that-could-have-been-an-email and the follow-up meeting are the exact artefacts the brief names — no generic "work is bad" grumble.
- **Fresh.** The cliche is not the punchline, it's the setup. The joke goes one step further: the email never even gets sent, it gets *scheduled* — a recursion the reader hasn't met before.
- **Tight.** One sentence, 115 characters. Nothing to explain, nothing to cut.

## Run it

```sh
python3 main.py
cat /output/entry.txt
```

`main.py` writes the single joke to `/output/entry.txt` (UTF-8, 1–200 characters, no network, no dependencies).
